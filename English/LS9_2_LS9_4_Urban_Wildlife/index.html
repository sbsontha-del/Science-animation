🐾 Urban Wildlife: Friend or Foe?

One small step at a time. Read → Think → Choose → Explain.

STEP 1 • SOURCE DETECTIVE
Who would you trust more?

Source A: A research paper written by scientists and published one year ago.

Source B: A social media post from an unknown person, posted two days ago.

💡 Ask yourself: Do I know who wrote it? Does the writer have expertise? Can the information be checked?
STEP 2 • THINK DEEPER
The social media post is newer. Does newer always mean more reliable?
🤔 Socratic thought: A source can be current but not necessarily reliable.
STEP 3 • LS 9.2
Read this short extract. Tap the strongest evidence.
Wild boars sometimes leave forests to look for food. This has occasionally led to road accidents and human-wildlife conflicts.
In one case, a woman attacked by a wild boar needed sixty stitches on her leg.
What does this evidence show?
🤔 Notice the word sometimes. Does one example prove that all wildlife is dangerous?
STEP 4 • LOOK AGAIN
Could humans be contributing to the problem?
Because some people feed macaques, they have learnt to associate humans with food.
They may grab plastic bags because they associate them with food. Their behaviour can be mistaken for aggression; macaques are generally not aggressive.
Sentence frame: This shows that human behaviour can contribute to conflict because ______.
STEP 5 • FIND THE VALUE
Do wild animals help the environment?
Macaques play an important role in dispersing seeds in nature reserves.
Monitor lizards help keep habitats clean and control populations of prey such as insects, crabs and fish.
Which conclusion is best supported?
STEP 6 • ANOTHER SOLUTION?
Must culling be the only solution?
Animal welfare groups argue that culling does not solve the problem. Another macaque group may take the place of one that is removed.
People should not feed wildlife and should keep a safe distance from wild animals.
What other solution does the text suggest?
STEP 7 • CHOOSE A OR B
Now that you have seen both sides, what do YOU think?
