Urban Wildlife: Friend or Foe?

Think like a detective. Question the source, examine the evidence, consider both sides, then make and defend your judgement.

🔎 Examine💭 Question⚖️ Decide📝 Justify
STAGE 1 • SOURCE DETECTIVE

Can we trust every source?

Before deciding whether urban wildlife are friends or foes, ask: Is the information reliable? Is it current?

Source A — Research paper

Wildlife in Singapore, Journal of Wildlife Studies. Written by scientists Sabrina Tan, Chin Seng Neo and Gretel Jabbar. Published one year ago.

Who wrote it? What expertise might they have? How recently was it published? Can the information be checked? Would you trust it? Why?
Source B — Social media post

Information from an unknown source, shared two days ago.

It is recent — but does recent automatically mean reliable? Who created the information? Can you verify the claim? What is the difference between current and reliable?
Source C — Wildlife protection book

Written by Professor A. Roshan, a professor studying wildlife, in 1985.

Does the author's expertise make the source reliable? Is information from 1985 current? Could recommendations have changed? Can a source be reliable but not current?
STAGE 2 • LS 9.2 CLOSE & CRITICAL READING

Consider BOTH positions

Position A — FOE

Urban wildlife are our foes and should be culled.

Position B — FRIEND

Urban wildlife are our friends and should not be culled.

Evidence 1 🐗

Wild boars sometimes leave forests in search of food. This has resulted in road accidents and human-wildlife conflicts. The reading describes a woman who was attacked and required sixty stitches.

Socratic question: Does one serious incident mean all urban wildlife should be culled? What else would you need to know?

Evidence 2 🐒

Human feeding can cause macaques to associate people with food. They may become bolder, enter homes or try to snatch plastic bags.

Socratic question: Is the animal the only problem, or could human behaviour be contributing?

Evidence 3 🌱 🦎

Macaques help disperse seeds. Monitor lizards help control populations of prey such as insects, crabs and fish.

Socratic question: What could happen to the environment if useful wildlife is removed?

Evidence 4 🤝

Animal welfare groups argue that culling may not solve the problem. If one macaque troop is removed, another may move in. People can instead avoid feeding wildlife, keep food away and maintain a safe distance.

Socratic question: If culling does not remove the cause of conflict, what other solutions could work?

⚖️ Now choose A or B

Which position do you think is better supported? There is no credit for simply clicking — you must defend your thinking.

Challenge yourself: What might someone who disagrees with you say?
Respond: After considering the other side, do you still agree with your position? Why?
